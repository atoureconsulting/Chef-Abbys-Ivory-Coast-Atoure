# Prompt: talent experience deck

Copy the prompt below, fill in the brackets, and attach the call notes and your media folder.

---

You are helping AToure (experience and logistics agency, Abidjan) pitch a visit by **[TALENT NAME]**, **[who they are in one line]**, to **[their management / client]**. They will be in **[CITY, COUNTRY]** from **[DATES]**. The goal is a short, persuasive deck that makes their team say yes. It is not a planning document.

**1. Research first, then tell me what you found.**
- Look up the talent: platforms and follower counts, credentials (verify every one), causes, past visits here and what they already filmed, so we don't repeat it.
- Look up each partner I name and suggest others who fit. For each, find **one stand-out fact that can be a headline**, the way a newspaper would title them (e.g. "The Ivorian who makes the 'best chocolate in the world'"), plus their social media stats.
- Flag anything you could not verify instead of writing around it.

**2. Build the story around people and activations, not logistics.**
- Each partner gets: who they are (short), why they matter (the headline fact), and **one creative activation with the talent that plays on that partner's theme**. Example: a chef with speed and endurance records gets a speed duel and an endurance relay.
- Explain every activation so someone with no context understands it. Never use a name like "L'Heure du Goûter" without saying what happens.
- Where it fits, connect the activation to the talent's own story or cause (their hometown, their charity, their country).
- Put the most emotional moment last.

**3. Keep it short.**
- **[4–7] slides.** One idea per slide. No filler slides (no "why now", no "what each side gets") unless I ask.
- Include: cover, partners and activations, institutional support (entities we are in contact with and what they could offer), what AToure handles, a **day-by-day activity plan** (Day 1 / 2 / 3 with activities, no hourly times, no arrival or airport lines), and a budget.

**4. Budget = logistics only unless I say otherwise.**
- Hotel, cars, security, airport welcome, data. No experience costs, no meals, no interpreter unless asked.
- Use real prices I give you (screenshots, quotes) over estimates. Show who we will ask to offset each line (ministries, embassies, sponsors).
- Add a plain timing warning: requests this close to the date aren't guaranteed. Each institution has its own process, and most here need at least a month.

**5. Be honest in the deck itself.**
- State clearly that these are potential activation ideas and that **none of the partners have been contacted yet** to confirm availability, participation or the concept.

**6. Design: made for this talent and this country, not our house style.**
- **Do not use the AToure template.** AToure appears only as a small "presented by" logo.
- Build a palette and type from the **country and the talent**. For Côte d'Ivoire: flag orange, green and ivory, with a warm text colour (e.g. cocoa). Pick a warm display font, a clean body font, and a handwritten accent font for notes.
- It must feel **welcoming and human, not AI-made**. Take inspiration from food, travel and lifestyle magazine decks:
  - Vary the layout on every slide: full-bleed photos, arched or rounded frames, a tilted print, round stickers, and a pattern from local textiles.
  - Avoid repeating templates, rows of identical cards, uppercase labels everywhere, emoji and generic icons.
- **No large empty areas. No overlapping text.** Render and check every slide before sending it to me.
- Titles in **English and simple** (e.g. "[Talent] in [Country]"), not a clever phrase in another language.
- Use my photos from the Drive folder **[FOLDER NAME]**. Crop so faces are never cut, and put the talent's best portrait on the cover.

**7. Working style.**
- Ask me only about real decisions (party size, which hotel, fee %). Decide the rest and tell me what you assumed.
- When I give feedback, apply it across the whole deck, not just the slide I mentioned.
